Careless driving ticket in Ontario: what it really costs, and whether to fight it

Don’t just pay it

Paying a careless driving ticket is a guilty plea. The $490 on the ticket is the smallest part of the cost — the conviction stays on your record and insurers treat it as a major conviction. You have 15 days to choose an option.

Drivee Editorial Updated October 8, 2026 7 min read

Careless driving is the charge police lay most often after a collision, and it is one of the most expensive tickets an Ontario driver can get — not because of the fine, but because of what the conviction does to your insurance. Here is what the charge actually is, what it costs, and how to decide what to do.

What “careless driving” actually means

The charge is under section 130 of the Highway Traffic Act: driving “without due care and attention” or “without reasonable consideration for other persons using the highway.”

It is deliberately broad. Officers commonly lay it after a rear-end collision, a crash while turning left, drifting out of a lane, or any collision where the cause looks like inattention. You don’t have to have hit anything — but most careless charges follow a collision.

For a conviction the prosecution has to prove your driving fell below the standard of a reasonably prudent driver in the circumstances. A collision alone is not proof of carelessness. That gap between “there was a crash” and “the driving was careless” is where most defences live.

The penalties

  • Set fine: $400. Most tickets show a total payable of about $490 once the victim fine surcharge and court costs are added.
  • 6 demerit points on your Ontario driving record.
  • If it goes to court, the range is $400 to $2,000, up to 6 months in jail, and a licence suspension of up to 2 years.

Six points is half of the 15 that trigger a suspension for a fully licensed driver, and new drivers on a G1 or G2 face action at far lower totals.

Careless driving causing bodily harm or death

If someone was hurt or killed, the charge is laid by summons, not a ticket you can pay, and you must go to court. The fine is $2,000 to $50,000, with up to 2 years in jail and a licence suspension of up to 5 years. Get legal representation.

What it does to your insurance

This is the real cost. Ontario insurers treat careless driving as a major conviction, and convictions are typically rated for about three years. Depending on your insurer and record, an increase of roughly $1,500 to $5,000 over that period is common (estimate — it varies widely). Some insurers decline to renew drivers with a major conviction at all.

If the charge came from a collision, the conviction comes on top of any at-fault claim — two separate hits to the same premium. Compare that with the typical cost of representation for this charge, roughly $700 to $2,500 (estimate), and fighting often pays for itself.

Your three options (you have 15 days)

The back of the ticket lists your options. Choose one within 15 days, or the court can convict you in your absence.

  1. Pay the fine. This is a guilty plea. You take the 6 points and the conviction.
  2. Early resolution meeting. You meet a prosecutor before any trial. Prosecutors sometimes agree to resolve the matter as a less serious offence with fewer points. Nothing is guaranteed.
  3. Request a trial. The prosecution must prove the charge. You (or your representative) can request disclosure — the officer’s notes, witness statements, any video — and test the evidence.

Is it worth fighting?

For most drivers, yes — it is one of the charges where getting help most often pays for itself, because the insurance cost dwarfs the fine. It is especially worth a second look if:

  • the collision was minor and nobody saw your driving before impact,
  • road, weather or another driver’s actions contributed,
  • you’re a new driver, or already have points, or
  • you drive for a living.

In Ontario, a licensed paralegal can represent you on a careless driving ticket. Many offer a free consultation, and your conversation with them is the best way to find out whether there is a defence in your particular case.

Common questions

How many demerit points is careless driving in Ontario?

Six demerit points on conviction.

How much is a careless driving ticket in Ontario?

The set fine is $400, and most tickets show a total payable of about $490 with the surcharge and costs. In court the fine can range from $400 to $2,000.

Will careless driving affect my insurance?

Yes. Insurers treat it as a major conviction, usually rated for about three years, and the increase often costs far more than the fine. Some insurers will not renew a policy after it.

Can careless driving be reduced to a lesser charge?

Sometimes. At an early resolution meeting or before trial, a prosecutor may agree to resolve it as a less serious offence with fewer points. It depends on the facts and the evidence, and nothing is guaranteed.

Can a paralegal fight a careless driving ticket?

Yes. Licensed paralegals in Ontario can represent drivers on Highway Traffic Act charges, including careless driving. If someone was injured or killed, the charge is far more serious and you should get legal advice right away.
